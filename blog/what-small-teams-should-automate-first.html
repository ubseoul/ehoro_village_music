What Small Teams Should Automate First

A simple way to choose your first automations, with a shortlist of tasks that usually pay off and a few to leave alone.

Ehoro Village · October 05, 2026 · 5 min read

Small teams feel the cost of repetitive work more than anyone, and they have the least time to experiment. The best first automations are boring, frequent and easy to check. Here is how to choose them.

Score each candidate

List the repetitive tasks your team does and rate each from 1 to 3 on four questions:

QuestionHigher score when…
How often?It happens daily or weekly
How long?It takes 30 minutes or more each time
How error-prone?Copying and retyping cause mistakes
How safe?A mistake is easy to catch and fix

Start with the highest total. If a task scores low on safety, it isn't a first automation, no matter how much time it would save.

Tasks that usually pay off first

  • Intake and triage: form submissions sorted, tagged and routed to the right person.
  • Recurring reports: the weekly or monthly summary someone rebuilds by hand.
  • Meeting follow-ups: notes turned into action items with owners and dates.
  • Reminders and follow-ups: overdue items, renewals, missing documents.
  • Moving data between tools: the copy-and-paste step between a form, a spreadsheet and an inbox.
  • First drafts of routine replies: reviewed by a person before sending.

Leave these for later

  • Anything involving legal, financial or eligibility decisions about a person.
  • Tasks that happen a few times a year.
  • Processes your team can't describe the same way twice.
  • Anything customer-facing without a review step.

Start small and prove it

Automate one task, run it alongside the manual process for two weeks, and compare. Once the team trusts it, retire the manual version and move to the next item on your list. Two or three reliable automations will do more for a small team than a dozen fragile ones. For the build itself, see How to Build AI Workflows People Actually Use.
